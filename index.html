About the Platform

Field Data That Drives Infrastructure Change

CycleAudit is Parisar's auditing tool for city teams. Surveyors on the ground document the real condition of cycle tracks. Every obstruction, broken surface, missing ramp, and dangerous intersection is recorded and scored, and each city's audit data stays with that city's leaders to review.

🗺️

Geo-Referenced Segments

Roads are divided into precise 500m segments with GPS coordinates, ensuring complete and consistent coverage.

📊

Multi-Dimensional Scoring

Safety, Continuity, and Comfort — three independent dimensions measured from real field observations and combined into a final weighted score.

📄

Professional PDF Reports

Auto-generate comprehensive stakeholder reports with segment scores, observations, and actionable recommendations, and download a city-wide score sheet.

🔒

Surveyor Accounts

Each audit is linked to a named surveyor on the city's audit team, enabling accountability and historical tracking of audit data over time.

🚴
Why Cycle Track Audits Matter
Cities invest heavily in cycle track infrastructure, yet many tracks stay underused or inaccessible because of obstructions, missing links, and unsafe crossings. Systematic audits create a factual baseline that city teams and Parisar can use to make the case for targeted repairs, encroachment removal, better lighting, and safer intersections.

Because every segment is audited the same way, results are consistent and comparable from one road, and one city, to the next.

Audit Process

How a Cycle Track Audit Works

Each audit is a project for one city. It moves through six steps, from set-up by Parisar to a published report anyone can read.

1
Admin

Parisar sets up the project

The Admin creates an audit project for a city and assigns it to that city's City Leader.

2
City Leader

The City Leader adds the roads

The City Leader picks the city's roads for this audit. Each road is split into segments automatically, each with its own start and end landmark.

3
City Leader

Surveyors are assigned

The City Leader gives each segment, or a whole road, to a surveyor on the city's audit team, then starts the audit.

4
Surveyor

Field audit of each segment

On the road, the surveyor fills in the segment form. The answers the score depends on are required, so no segment can be submitted half-complete. A submitted segment is locked and scored automatically.

5
City Leader

Review and send to the Admin

The City Leader reviews every submitted segment, approving it or sending it back to the surveyor with a note. Once all segments are approved, the audit is closed and sent to the Admin.

6
Admin

Approval and publication

The Admin approves the audit and it is published under Audit Data on this page, with an online report and a PDF download. If something needs fixing, the Admin returns it to the City Leader with a note.

Per segment

Start and end landmarks, GPS, buffer zone, lighting, surface, shade, obstructions, intersections, footpath and surveyor comments.

Locked after submission

A submitted segment cannot be quietly changed. It only reopens if the City Leader sends it back.

Road scores

A road's score is its segment scores weighted by segment length.

Scoring Methodology

How Scores Are Calculated

Each segment receives three independent scores — Safety, Continuity, and Comfort — which are combined into a final score from 0 to 100. The lower the score, the better the segment.

Rating scale lower is better
0 · no problems found100 · worst
Good0–20Safe and comfortable for everyday cycling
OK21–40Usable, with minor problems
Poor41–60Noticeable problems for cyclists
Bad61–80Serious problems, hard to use safely
Very Bad81–100Unsafe or unusable for most cyclists
A segment's score measures the problems found across Safety, Continuity and Comfort.
